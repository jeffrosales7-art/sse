### Case 1: Adaptive Leadership (coach case)

❓ **Licensing.** This case uses *Deborah DiSanzo at Philips Medical Systems (A)* (Babson College / Harvard Business Publishing), licensed for your use. **The case PDF will NOT be uploaded to Wix.** Each cohort participant needs their own licensed copy, which you provide outside Wix. The step keeps your own scenario summary and questions, with the participant-specific references made generic.

- **Scenario:** Read the case study I sent you: Deborah DiSanzo at Philips Medical Systems (A), Babson College. In 2006 Deborah leaves a fast-growing unit she built to lead Patient Monitoring: a profitable market leader with 1,500 people in the US and Germany, but with slowing growth, new low-cost competitors and 13 general managers in 19 years. The senior team is experienced and confident, works in silos, and has no shared strategy. As the newcomer she has to earn credibility, find out what is really going on, and get the team to agree on one purpose and one direction. ✏️ As you read, keep your own team in mind: where direction is unclear, where demands compete, and where you still do the work instead of directing it. *(was: "your own Studio … requests from many countries")*
- **Questions:**
  1. **Technical or adaptive?** List the main challenges Deborah faced when she took over. Which could be solved by expertise or a decision from the top, and which needed people to change how they think and work together? Explain your choice.
  2. **Earning credibility.** What did Deborah do in her first months to win over a team that had seen 13 general managers come and go? Which two actions mattered most, and why?
  3. **Diagnosing before acting.** She asked 85 people what the strategy was and got 87 different answers. What does that tell you about the organization? ✏️ If you asked your team and the people who depend on it what your team is for, how many different answers would you get?
  4. **Giving the work back.** In the three-day meeting in Germany, how did she get strong, long-serving experts to create one purpose and a four-point strategy together, instead of handing them her own? What did she do, and what did she hold back from doing?
  5. **Making it stick.** Afterwards she gave each objective an owner, shared progress dashboards with the whole team, and repeated the strategy in every meeting. ✏️ Which of these would help your team take ownership without you stepping in? What would you adapt for your team?
  6. **Your adaptive challenge.** ✏️ Name one challenge in your team right now that a new process or tool alone will not fix. Who needs to do the work of changing, and what is your role as the leader?
  7. **Your next step.** What is one thing you will do in the next two weeks, inspired by this case, that moves you from doing to directing? What will you see if it works?

## Leadership diagnostic (start and end)

All 30 items are kept **unchanged** (already generic): 5 areas, 5 rating items (1–5) plus 1 written question each:

1. Moving from doing to directing (P1–P6)
2. Delegation and ownership (P7–P12)
3. Managing priorities and saying no (P13–P18)
4. Providing clear direction (P19–P24)
5. Follow-through and accountability (P25–P30)

## Final reflection (6 questions)

1. What are the most important shifts you have made as a leader during this program?
2. Which of the five priorities have you improved most? Share a specific example from work.
3. Which tool or idea from the modules have you used most, and what result did it bring?
4. ✏️ What feedback have you received from your team, peers or manager about your leadership? *(was: "director")*
5. Which priority still needs the most attention, and what will you do about it in the next 90 days?
6. ✏️ What support do you need from your manager or sponsor to keep growing? *(was: "your director")*
