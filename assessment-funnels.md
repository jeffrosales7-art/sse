# Assessment Funnels — coachjeffrosales.com

Two lead-generation funnels, one per program that has no clients yet:

| Funnel | Program it feeds | Audience |
|---|---|---|
| **New Manager Readiness Assessment** | Emerging Manager Accelerator™ | Newly promoted / soon-to-be managers (and HR buying for them) |
| **Workplace Readiness Assessment** | Early Career Foundation™ | Graduating students, fresh graduates, parents, university staff |

**How each funnel works**

1. Visitor lands on a short assessment page (from Google, LinkedIn, Facebook, the program page, or a referral).
2. Answers 10 statements on a 1–5 scale (about 3 minutes) plus a few quick profile questions.
3. Enters first name + email to receive their result.
4. Gets their result immediately by email, then 3 short follow-up emails over ~10 days.
5. Every email ends with one action: **Book a free discovery call → coachjeffrosales.com/book**.

**Scale used in both assessments** (how often is this true for you?)
1 = Rarely · 2 = Occasionally · 3 = Sometimes · 4 = Often · 5 = Almost always
Total score = sum of the 10 answers (10–50).

## Funnel 1 — New Manager Readiness Assessment

### Landing page

**Label:** FREE · 3 MINUTES · INSTANT RESULTS
**Headline:** How ready are you to lead a team?
**Subheadline:** Most new managers are promoted for being great at their job, then measured by what their team delivers. This 3-minute assessment shows where you stand on the five shifts that matter most, with practical next steps.
**Who it's for (bullets):**
- You were promoted to manager in the last two years
- You expect to lead people soon
- You're in HR or L&D and want a quick read on emerging managers
**Button:** Start the Assessment
**Small print:** Your answers are confidential and used only to send your results and occasional leadership tips. Unsubscribe anytime.

### Profile questions (before the statements)

1. **Where are you right now?** — About to become a manager / Manager for less than 1 year / Manager for 1–3 years / HR or L&D, assessing for my team
2. **How many people do you lead (or will you lead)?** — None yet / 1–3 / 4–8 / 9 or more
3. **What's your biggest leadership challenge right now?** (short text, optional)

### The 10 statements

| # | Area | Statement |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Mindset shift | I measure my success by what my team achieves, not only by my own output. |
| 2 | Mindset shift | When a team member is struggling, I coach them instead of jumping in to do the work myself. |
| 3 | Delegation | I delegate work with a clear outcome, a deadline and the authority to make decisions. |
| 4 | Delegation | I follow up on delegated work without micromanaging. |
| 5 | Feedback | I give specific, timely feedback, both positive and corrective. |
| 6 | Feedback | I address performance issues early instead of hoping they resolve themselves. |
| 7 | Team clarity | Everyone on my team knows their priorities and how their work connects to our goals. |
| 8 | Team clarity | I hold regular one-on-ones that focus on the person, not just task updates. |
| 9 | Self-management | I protect time in my week for leading (planning, coaching, thinking), not just doing. |
| 10 | Influence | I can influence my peers and my own manager to get support and resources for my team. |

### Results

**10–24 · The Expert in Transition**
You're still leading mostly through your own expertise, which is completely normal early on, and exactly where many new managers get stuck. The biggest gains will come from shifting how you define success and from delegating with clarity.
*Try this week:* Pick one task you usually do yourself and delegate it with a clear outcome, a deadline and the decision rights. Then resist taking it back.

**25–37 · The Developing Leader**
You've made the shift in some areas but not yet in all of them. You likely have a few strong habits (often feedback or one-on-ones) and a few gaps that cost you time and energy every week.
*Try this week:* Look at your two lowest-scoring areas. Choose one, and set a single, visible habit for it, for example a weekly 20-minute one-on-one with each team member.

**38–50 · Ready to Accelerate**
You have solid leadership foundations. Your next step is moving from good habits to a repeatable leadership system, and building influence beyond your own team.
*Try this week:* Ask two team members and one peer: "What's one thing I could do to make your work easier?" Act on one answer and tell them you did.

## Funnel 2 — Workplace Readiness Assessment (fresh graduates)

### Landing page

**Label:** FREE · 3 MINUTES · INSTANT RESULTS
**Headline:** Are you ready for your first job?
**Subheadline:** A degree gets you hired. Professional maturity keeps you there. Find out how ready you are for the realities of work in five areas employers care about, with practical tips to close the gaps.
**Who it's for (bullets):**
- Graduating students and fresh graduates
- Young professionals in their first one to two years at work
- Parents and university staff supporting a graduate
**Button:** Start the Assessment
**Small print:** Your answers are confidential and used only to send your results and occasional career tips. Unsubscribe anytime.

### Profile questions (before the statements)

1. **Which best describes you?** — Graduating within 12 months / Graduated less than 1 year ago / Working for 1–2 years / Parent of a student or graduate / University staff
2. **What's your field of study or work?** (short text, optional)
3. **What worries you most about starting work?** (short text, optional)

*(If "Parent" or "University staff" is selected, show a note: "Answer the statements as you think your graduate or student would.")*

### The 10 statements

| # | Area | Statement |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ownership | I finish what I commit to without needing reminders. |
| 2 | Ownership | When I make a mistake, I own it and fix it instead of explaining it away. |
| 3 | Communication | I write clear, professional emails and messages. |
| 4 | Communication | I can explain my ideas confidently in meetings or presentations. |
| 5 | Self-management | I plan my week and meet deadlines without last-minute rushes. |
| 6 | Self-management | I stay composed and focused when I'm under pressure. |
| 7 | Working with others | I ask for feedback and act on it, even when it's uncomfortable. |
| 8 | Working with others | I work well with people whose style or background is different from mine. |
| 9 | Career direction | I know what kind of role and workplace fits my strengths and values. |
| 10 | Career direction | I can clearly describe what I offer an employer, with real examples. |

### Results

**10–24 · Getting Started**
You're at the beginning of the move from student to professional, and that's okay. Most graduates are never taught these skills directly. Building a few habits now will make your first year much easier.
*Try this week:* Choose one commitment you've made (to a teacher, a group or a family member) and deliver it early, without a reminder. Notice how people respond.

**25–37 · Building Foundations**
You have real strengths, and some clear gaps that employers will notice in your first months. The good news: these are learnable, and small habits make a big difference.
*Try this week:* Look at your two lowest-scoring areas. Ask someone who knows you well for one specific example of how you could improve in one of them.

**38–50 · Workplace Ready**
You already show many of the behaviors employers expect from day one. Your next step is direction: being clear about where you want to grow, and how to describe your value.
*Try this week:* Write three sentences that answer "What do I offer an employer?", each with a real example. Practice saying them out loud.
